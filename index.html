I study how the structure of information determines what can be learned, computed, and verified. My work draws on mathematical optimization, information theory, signal processing, and machine learning.

A central theme of my research is the reliability of intelligent systems. In radar, critical decisions about the physical world depend on the quality of sensing and inference. This same concern motivates my work on AI safety, particularly on mathematical guarantees for learning and computation.

Research & selected papers

Selected work on the relationship between information, computational difficulty, and the reliability of learning and decision-making.

Sample abundance and tractability

Our work on sample abundance shows how sufficiently rich measurements, even at low resolution, can render difficult optimization constraints redundant, shifting the computational regime from costly optimization to simple linear feasibility.

  • Harnessing the Power of Sample Abundance: Theoretical Guarantees and Algorithms for Accelerated One-Bit SensingA. Eamaz, F. Yeganegi, D. Needell, M. Soltanalian · IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, 2024
  • One-Bit Phase Retrieval: More Samples Means Less Complexity?A. Eamaz, F. Yeganegi, M. Soltanalian · IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing, 2022

Certification of neural-network training

A flat training curve does not establish that an optimizer has found a good solution. Training Under Challenge constructs competing models in a declared class and compares their objective values. Better alternatives expose suboptimality; global optimality-gap bounds require justified coverage conditions.

  • Training Under Challenge: Executable Certificates and Challenge-Closed Optimality for Neural NetworksF. Yeganegi, A. Eamaz, M. Soltanalian · arXiv, 2026 (preprint)
  • Trust, but Verify: Peeling Low-Bit Transformer Networks for Training MonitoringA. Eamaz, F. Yeganegi, M. Soltanalian · arXiv, 2026 (preprint)

Radar, sensing, and critical decisions

In critical sensing applications, a system must establish what is present and where it is, often under severe time, hardware, and information constraints. We develop waveform design, adaptive sensing, optimization, and inference methods to improve the information available for these decisions, and establish mathematical guarantees for their performance.

  • Guaranteed Deep Learning for Reliable Radar Signal ProcessingS. Khobahi, A. Mostajeran, M. Emadi, P. Wang, M. Soltanalian · IEEE SAM, 2022 (invited)
  • Designing Unimodular Codes Via Quadratic OptimizationM. Soltanalian, P. Stoica · IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing, 2014
  • Beyond Semidefinite Relaxation: Basis Banks and Computationally Enhanced GuaranteesM. Soltanalian, B. Hassibi · ISIT, 2015

See also Signal Design for Modern Radar Systems (2022).

Quantized neural computation

When can greater depth compensate for limited arithmetic precision? We study this tradeoff for quantized residual computation, including structural limits and the effect of rounding. Related work develops behavioral certificates for quantized language models, bounding the risk of prediction changes under permitted weight modifications and the population loss of the deployed model.

  • When Can Depth Replace Precision? A Resource Theory of Quantized Neural ComputationM. Soltanalian · arXiv, 2026 (preprint)
  • Behavioral Capacity Certificates for Quantized Language ModelsA. Eamaz, M. Soltanalian · arXiv, 2026 (preprint)

Learning and adaptation at scale

We develop efficient methods for adapting language models, including RoCoFT, which updates selected rows or columns of weight matrices. On Llama 2–13B, its column-update variant outperformed LoRA on 10 of 13 reasoning benchmarks while using 45% fewer trainable parameters. Our related analysis of regularized fine-tuning connects adaptation performance and layer selection to the spectrum of the neural tangent kernel.

  • Linearization Explains Fine-Tuning in Large Language ModelsZ. Rahimi Afzal, T. Esmaeilbeig, M. Soltanalian, M. I. Ohannessian · NeurIPS, 2025
  • RoCoFT: Efficient Finetuning of Large Language Models with Row-Column UpdatesM. Kowsher, T. Esmaeilbeig, C.-N. Yu, C. Chen, M. Soltanalian, N. Yousefi · ACL, 2025

Mathematical foundations of intelligence

Why can finite intelligence succeed without retaining a complete description of its environment? I study the minimum information that must be retained to preserve the quality of decisions across families of future tasks. Shared structure among tasks, rather than the difficulty of each task alone, can determine this information requirement.

  • Why Finite Intelligence Works in an Infinite World: A Mathematical TheoryM. Soltanalian · arXiv, 2026 (preprint)

About

Background

I joined UIC in 2015, following research appointments at Caltech with Babak Hassibi and at the Interdisciplinary Centre for Security, Reliability and Trust (SnT), University of Luxembourg, with Björn Ottersten. I received my Ph.D. from Uppsala University under Peter (Petre) Stoica and my undergraduate degree from Sharif University of Technology.

Professional service

I chair the IEEE Signal Processing Society Chicago Chapter and have served on the Society’s Sensor Array and Multichannel Technical Committee and as an associate editor of three scientific journals, including IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing. My reviewer and program committee service includes NeurIPS, ICLR, and AISTATS. I have also served on grant review panels and reviewed proposals for multiple national and international research funding agencies.
